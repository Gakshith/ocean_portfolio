**Research, in brief.** Jurors in 2026 reward one point of view carried through every choice. They mark down motion without meaning and any 3D that drops frames on a mid-range Android, and they score reduced-motion fallbacks ([hontran.dev, a juror's 2026 list](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). The looks now called generic are dark mode by default, mono "technical" labels on everything, and a serif chosen by reflex ([Envato 2026 portfolio trends](https://elements.envato.com/learn/portfolio-trends), [Teleanu, AI-era aesthetics 2026](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/aesthetics-in-the-ai-era-visual-web-design-trends-for-2026-5a0f75a10e98)).
**Originality check.** I found no site that uses an engineered (goal-based) caustic to write a name. The prior art is Ferraro's physical lenses ([Magic Windows](https://mattferraro.dev/posts/caustics-engineering)) and ordinary decorative caustics ([three.js WebGPU caustics](https://threejs.org/examples/webgpu_caustics.html), [Maxime Heckel](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/caustics-in-webgl/), [Codrops, Volatile Nexus, Aug 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/08/31/volatile-nexus-tinkering-with-glass-caustics-cubes-and-sound-in-three-js/)). The hero idea is original. What does not stand out is the look of S2–S7 and the layout of S3–S6.

---

## Verdict

The concept wins a Site of the Day on its own: light refracted through designed water really does print his name. The execution does not yet. At 1440×900 the proof crops the name, the die never gets dark enough to show his name clearly, and after the hero the site becomes a dark editorial template: a text column beside a widget box, four times over, while the recruiter still does not know what he built in the first screen. **The single biggest fix:** use the first viewport to answer "what did he build". Put a four-line project index in the ink plate, cut the S1 pin to about 120vh, and frame the focus so the whole name is visible and clearly brighter than the fill around it.

## Scores

Scale 1–10. **Overall 6.1 / 10.** It is not yet an award-level submission, and the ceiling is a 9 if the majors are fixed.

- **Concept 8.** Caustics as lithography is physically true, specific to chip design, and nobody else could use it; the atoll with the I/O pads as contact is exact. One point is lost because the die, carrying his name and his projects, reads as a chip he taped out (C-17).
- **Story 6.** S1 and S7 bracket the site well. S3–S6 repeat the same beat with the same staging, and S6 (sonar rings) is the weakest, most familiar image on the site.
- **UX 6.** The fast path, Still mode, keyboard model and DOM twins are excellent on paper. But no project name appears in the first viewport, the hero is 244vh long, and the Surface Interrupt proof cannot be closed on a phone.
- **Typography 6.** Newsreader and Martian Mono are well specified. The hero letterforms, the most-seen type on the site, read as an 8-bit arcade font, and the style tile and the proof draw two different glyph sets.
- **Composition 5.** The proof crops the name at the hold. The upper-left 40% of the hero is dead black at P=0. The S7 labelled leads fall below the fold at 1440×900.
- **Originality 7.** The hero is new. The rest is the 2025–26 "dark ground, cream serif, uppercase mono labels, one warm accent" look.
- **Motion 6.** Motion is disciplined and every move has a mechanism, which is rare. The focus proof works and runs its real refraction. The downsides: the hold frame is low-contrast and cropped, the S6 motion shows the wrong physics, and 03 has internal contradictions.
- **Details 7.** The work is strong: a real ADV_IND with 17/31 bytes and a CRC, the style tile's correct load-use cycle table, CSA#1 lanes in frequency order, and a hatch plus ✕ rather than colour alone. Some mechanism errors remain (C-12, C-13).
- **Execution / feasibility 5.** The heightfield format fails (a 16-bit PNG is decoded to 8 bits). The frozen 1024² RT goes soft at block dwell. Three live sims plus an offline solve is a lot of build for one student portfolio.
- **Accessibility 7.** The spec is best-in-class: focus management, a live reduced-motion listener, `inert`, WCAG 2.5.7 alternatives, and contrast checked against the worst frame. The proofs lag behind it: no close control, no focus trap, and the Contact half of the pill is hidden on phones.
- **Performance risk 5** (10 = safe). The full-quality caustic is about 1.57M triangles per frame, which fails on Adreno 610 / Mali-G57. The page is about 1 MB before 3D if shipped naively. S2–S7 and S7 are cheap.

## Findings

C-01 [blocker] owner: ux-designer — S1-hero / 02-ux "User flow 1": the first viewport says who he is but not **what he built**. The project index ("On this die") only arrives in S2, after a 244vh S1 section (180vh pin). On a phone (`s1-phone-hold.png`, `tile-phone.png`) the first screen shows his name **twice**, once in light and once as the h1, and nothing else. The 30-second test depends on the recruiter scrolling through the whole hero.
Why it matters: the brief's primary audience scans; "what he built" is a third of the pass criteria and must not sit 2.4 screens down.
Fix: add a 4-line index to the ink plate under the role line, as DOM links in `--t-data`: `BLE Link Layer — senior design, in progress · RISC-V 5-stage pipeline — hazard debug, review, docs · WiSARD RAM-based classifier · CyBot autonomous robot`. When the ADV_IND panel collapses, the index takes its place. Cut the S1 pin to 120vh on desktop and 100vh on phone (focus P 0.05–0.55, rise 0.55–1.0), and bring S2's headline in by P 0.7. On the phone, show the light-name only after the first scroll; before that the top 55% is shimmer, so the name appears once.

C-02 [blocker] owner: motion-designer — S1-hero proof, the hold (`s1-p03.png`, `s1-p052.png` at 1440×900): the light-name is **cropped by the right viewport edge**, with the last U of GOJURU and part of AKASH cut off, and the amber pill overlaps the die's top-right corner. The lens-shift framing sizes the near shot on a 1.46×0.74 box that doesn't fit the free region. At 375×667 the die also runs past both edges.
Why it matters: the signature moment is "the light focused into his name", and it currently prints a clipped name.
Fix: compute the near framing from the lettering's bounding box plus a 6% safe margin, **excluding** the 56px bar and pill zone, and clamp so the whole word block always fits the free region. Add a framing test to the proof that asserts the projected letter bbox is inside the viewport at 1280×720, 1440×900, 1920×1080, 375×667 and 390×844, and screenshot each size.

C-03 [major] owner: art-director — S1-hero / 04-art "The exposure": the promised "exposed plate" does not happen. At f=1 (`s1-p052.png`, `s1-p1.png`, `s1-reduced.png`) the ground between strokes is mid-tan, and the pad ring and the 7px fill squares are as bright as or brighter than the letters. The name has the **lowest contrast** of anything on the die. The target luminances in 04/03 (pads 0.8, fill 0.45 on a dense 18px pitch, traces 1.0) give the light almost nowhere dark to leave from.
Why it matters: the palette's whole logic (sunlit sand → dark plate by energy conservation) and the legibility of the name both depend on this contrast; right now the die reads as a lit waffle.
Fix: rebalance the target so the name dominates. Traces 1.0; pads 0.35; seal ring 0.3; fill 0.12 at a 24px pitch with a 20px keep-out around the letters; core 0.02. Acceptance test in the proof: at f=1, the mean luminance of the letter mask is at least 3× the mean of the fill region and at least 8× the core. Coordinate with motion-designer, who owns the solve (C-08).

C-04 [major] owner: art-director — S1-hero / S7-contact type-as-image: the Manhattan glyphs on the 4×6 grid read as an **8-bit arcade / LCD font**. The K is a staircase, the J is a hook, and R and K are hard to tell apart at a glance (`tile-hero.png`, `s7-desk.png`). There are also **two different glyph sets**: `style-tile.html` defines K, G, J and R differently from `motion-S1-hero.html` and `motion-S7-contact.html`.
Why it matters: this is the most-seen type on the site and the image jurors will screenshot; "retro pixel logo" undercuts "chip designer's top-metal art".
Fix: draw one canonical glyph set as vector, on a finer routing grid (8 tracks × 12, still 90° only). Use real metal-width variation: wide straps for stems and minimum width for jogs. Replace diagonals with 1-track jogs, the way a router would, and keep vias only at stroke ends. Put it in one file (`glyphs.json`) that feeds the solve target, the stills and the S2/S7 SVG. Check legibility at 24px cap height on phone.

C-05 [major] owner: motion-designer — S1-hero proof at P=0 (`s1-p0.png`): the lit floor is a finite 2×2 patch. Under the 28° tilt it shows as a trapezoid with a hard diagonal edge, and the **upper-left 40% of the frame is empty black**. That contradicts 04-art ("the upper left third is sand and shimmer", "it opens on a sunlit plate: pale wet sand"). The opening sand also renders dark brown, not pale.
Why it matters: the first frame is the site's poster; a floating textured card on black reads as a demo, not as a sea floor.
Fix: the solve is already periodic (FFT), so tile the caustic RT (RepeatWrapping) across a floor at least 4× larger than the frustum at 28°, with the die in the centre tile only. At f=0, raise the ambient to the sunlit value (albedo × 0.35) and ramp it to 0.07 with `f`, so the opening is pale sand and the darkening is visibly the exposure. Re-shoot P=0 at 1440 and 375.

C-06 [major] owner: ux-designer — S3–S6 / 02-ux "Shared layout: the sticky split": four consecutive sections (about 620vh, most of the site) use one template: text in columns 1–4 and an opaque 2D stage in columns 5–12, "deliberate calm repetition". The stage sits **over** the 3D block, so the WebGL world is visible only as a strip of routing around a DOM box and during camera travel. A juror will see a well-built document with a WebGL background.
Why it matters: the brief asks for "one world", and jurors score "decorated templates" mid-range; this is where Creativity and Design points are lost.
Fix: keep the DOM controls, DOM twins and focus model exactly as specified, but **draw each sim onto the block**: its canvas becomes a `CanvasTexture` on the block's top-metal plane in the scene (in Still mode the same canvas sits flat in the DOM). Then vary the framing per block. S3 is a full-width spectrogram band across the radio block, with text above. S4 is the five chambers laid along the CPU block, with the cycle table below. S5 is a centred square pool with the discriminator bars to the right. Only S6 keeps the side column.

C-07 [major] owner: motion-designer — S2–S5 camera dwell / 03-motion "Performance": "the caustic RT is frozen and the camera travels over a static texture" gives a 1024² RT, so a block is about 255 texels. On a 700 CSS px stage at DPR 2 that is about 5.5 device px per texel, and about 11 per *solved* texel (the solve is 512²). Edges will blur over 20–30 px. 04-art's "specimens under a coaxial microscope" can't be sharp this way.
Why it matters: the close-ups are where engineers look hardest, and softness reads as low quality.
Fix: leave the light soft, but render the **metal layout** (block rows, straps, block outlines, layer colours) procedurally in the floor shader, or as vector/SDF, so it stays crisp at any zoom. Alternatively, bake 2048² crops per stop offline as lazily loaded KTX2 files. Don't re-render the caustic at a higher resolution: intensity is flat per triangle, so you would see facets.

C-08 [major] owner: motion-designer — SM-1 performance: full quality is 512² × 3 dispersion passes, about 1.57M triangles per frame into a 1024² RGBA16F RT with mips every frame. Estimated at 8–12 ms for the caustic alone on Adreno 610 / Mali-G57 (the proof's 60 fps is display-capped on an M-series Mac). The phone tier (256² × 1 pass) is estimated at 3–8 ms, which is fine, but 03 only uses it as a fallback cut.
Why it matters: jurors test on mid-range Android; the one medium effect has to be the smoothest thing on the site.
Fix: make the phone budget the **default** for `pointer: coarse`. Choose the tier with a 10-frame offscreen warm-up while the ADV_IND panel shows, never in the middle of the focus. Replace the 3-pass dispersion on all tiers with 3 offset samples of one RT in the floor shader (the 8% chroma looks the same). Add `+1e-8` to the area-ratio denominator so mediump precision can't produce NaN that spreads through the mips.

C-09 [major] owner: motion-designer — 03-motion SM-1 "Production": "ships as `h` in a 16-bit PNG (~300 KB)". Browsers decode PNGs to 8 bits per channel when uploading to a texture. Slope is a derivative of height and intensity is a second derivative, so 8-bit steps become visible caustic noise.
Why it matters: the production hero would not match the proof.
Fix: bake and ship the **slope** field directly as RG16F (KTX2 or a raw Float16 `.bin`, about 1 MB, gzipped). Or pack the high and low bytes into an RGBA8 PNG uploaded with nearest filtering, `premultiplyAlpha: false` and `colorSpace: NoColorSpace`. Show it with a side-by-side screenshot against the Worker solve.

C-10 [major] owner: motion-designer — S7-contact proof (`s7-desk.png`): at 1440×900 the 512×640 SVG stage is about 950px tall, so the **bond wires, leads and the RESUME/EMAIL/… labels (the SM-2 payoff) fall below the fold**, and the die sits centred with its lettering instead of its reef. On a phone (`s7-phone.png`) the lead labels are about 6px text and the pad list starts below the fold.
Why it matters: the resolve beat lands where the user must act, and its payoff can't be seen there.
Fix: frame S7 on the **south half** of the die: the reef, the five contact pads, the bond wires and the labelled leads, all within `100vh − 56px` at 1280×720 and up. Show the name only as a cropped glow at the top. Make lead labels DOM chips at `--t-label` 12px. On phone, cap the stage at 32vh so at least the first two pad rows are visible on arrival.

C-11 [major] owner: motion-designer — Surface Interrupt in `motion-S1-hero.html` (`s1-phone-irq.png`): the only way to close it is `Esc`, so a **phone user cannot close it** (it has no ✕ and no "Back to where you were"). `main` is not `inert` and focus is not trapped. The pill stays live above the sheet. All four links are equal amber buttons, so Resume is not the primary one.
Why it matters: this is the site's fast path; a proof that traps phone users will be built that way if nobody fixes the proof.
Fix: bring the proof in line with 02-ux: a 44px ✕ close button and a "Back to where you were" button, `inert` on everything outside the dialog, a focus trap, `pushState` so Back closes it, and the pill hidden while it is open. Make "Open resume (PDF) ↗" the only amber fill. Email, LinkedIn and GitHub become `--floor-2` buttons with a `--line-strong` border.

C-12 [major] owner: ux-designer — S3-linklayer "Operating Hop": "mark a lane bad … the map remaps live" is wrong for a live connection, and it is exactly Akash's area (connection handling). In BLE the central sends `LL_CHANNEL_MAP_IND`, and the new map takes effect at a future connection-event *instant*. CSA#1 also needs at least **2 used channels**, and the next hop is computed from `lastUnmappedChannel`, not from the remapped channel shown in "Last 8 hops".
Why it matters: a BLE engineer will catch it, and the brief promises zero wrong terms.
Fix: tapping a lane stages a pending map. The formula line prints `LL_CHANNEL_MAP_IND · instant = event n+6`, a countdown runs, and the map switches at that event. Disable marking a chip bad when only 2 used channels are left, and explain why inline. Label the formula `(lastUnmapped + hop) mod 37`, and show both unmapped and mapped channels in the hop log (`19→19`, `14→ch3`).

C-13 [major] owner: ux-designer — S4-riscv: the 02-ux stage wireframe shows `lw x5` in MEM, the bubble in EX and the arrow "forward MEM/WB → EX" **in the same cycle**. In that cycle EX holds the bubble, and the forward happens in the *next* cycle (the style tile gets it right: "cycle 5"). The wireframe's instruction order (`add` in WB, `or` in IF) also doesn't match the style tile's `lw/add/sub` table. The pipeline assumptions are not stated anywhere: where branches resolve (EX gives 2 flushed instructions, ID gives 1) and whether the register file writes before it reads (this changes the no-forwarding stall count from 3 to 2).
Why it matters: the cycle table "is what engineers actually read"; one wrong arrow costs the section its credibility.
Fix: redraw the wireframe from the style tile's run (lw/add/sub, bubble in EX at cycle 4, MEM/WB→EX at cycle 5). Add one data line on the stage: `Textbook 5-stage · branch resolves in EX · register file writes, then reads · a teaching model, not our RTL`. Generate the cycle table from the same JS model that drives the animation, so the two can't drift apart.

C-14 [major] owner: motion-designer — S6-cybot: the ping rings expand **360°** from the robot. The PING))) ultrasonic sensor is a forward cone (roughly ±20°), not an omnidirectional sonar. Expanding rings in dark water is also the stock submarine-sonar image, the most familiar picture on the site. Bump and IR, two of the three sensors named in content.md, are missing.
Why it matters: wrong physics in the one section about a robot he actually built, and the weakest beat in the story.
Fix: draw each ping as a narrow cone along the robot's heading, and draw the echo only where the cone meets an obstacle. Show IR as a short-range dotted ray and bump as a contact tick on the robot outline, so all three sensors appear. Make no servo-sweep claim unless Akash confirms it (`[SLOT: cybot-sensing-detail]`).

C-15 [major] owner: art-director — GLOBAL look after S1 (`tile-a.jpg`, `tile-b.jpg`, `tile-c.jpg`): a warm-black ground, cream serif, tracked uppercase mono labels on every rail and chip, and one amber accent. That is the 2025–26 "tasteful dark editorial" default that jurors and trend write-ups now file as generic. The sun-print idea is visible only in S1 and in the S5 pool; S2–S7 could belong to any dev portfolio.
Why it matters: the originality score comes from the concept, and the rest of the site doesn't carry it.
Fix: give S2–S7 a **photomask grammar** in place of generic hairlines. Section breaks become scribe lanes with real reticle marks (alignment keys, a die-ID string such as `AG-S4 · CPU`). Rails become reticle-edge labels. Let the plate darken **progressively** down the page: S2 keeps a 12% warm residual exposure and S6 is full black, so the exposure is a journey, not a switch. Limit uppercase mono to data and pads, and set nav and rails in Newsreader small caps.

C-16 [major] owner: art-director — style tile at 375px (`tile-phone.png`): `.pill .con{display:none}` **removes Contact** below 520px, and the page overflows horizontally (scrollWidth 378 in a 375 viewport). Both motion proofs also put a single pill top-right on phone. 02-ux specifies a bottom bar with `[≡ 3/7] [Resume ↗] [Contact]`.
Why it matters: on phones, the likeliest device from a LinkedIn link, the contact route vanishes from the tile the build will copy.
Fix: build the 02-ux phone bottom bar in the style tile (56px plus safe area, the three targets at 144/88/96px) and remove the top-right pill on phone. Find and fix the 3px overflow (the hero SVG or the proof control) and assert `scrollWidth === innerWidth` at 375.

C-17 [major] owner: creative-director — S1/S2/S7 copy and honesty: a die with AKASH GOJURU on top metal, a pad ring and his four projects as blocks, captioned "These are mine", will read to a hardware recruiter as **a chip he taped out**. content.md supports no tape-out, and the RISC-V block's RTL was his partner's.
Why it matters: the first interview question will be "tell me about your tape-out"; the brief forbids implied achievements.
Fix: add one plain caption beside the S2 die and in the "How the light works" footer: "An illustrative die. The blocks are my projects; it is not a fabricated chip." Change the S7 line to "Every chip talks to the world through its pads. Here are mine to you:" or similar, and keep "mine" about the links, not the silicon.

C-18 [major] owner: creative-director — S1 plate and the Surface Interrupt: recruiters need **graduation term / availability**, and it appears nowhere. The role line also differs between files: "Heading into ASIC and chip design." (S1 proof) vs "Seeking hardware / chip-design internships." (02-ux, style tile).
Why it matters: availability decides whether a recruiter can act, and inconsistent role lines break the 30-second read.
Fix: freeze one role line built from content.md and vision words, for example "Computer Engineering, Iowa State. Heading into ASIC design; seeking hardware and chip-design internships." Add `[SLOT: grad-term]` (for example "Graduating <term>") to the plate and the interrupt, and ask Akash for it; do not infer it from the senior-design dates.

C-19 [minor] owner: motion-designer — S3-linklayer motion: a "collision" (a hop that lands on a whale-covered lane still in use) turns the mark "hatched ✕", the same treatment 02-ux uses for a channel the user **marked bad**. That merges two states: packet lost, and channel excluded from the map.
Why it matters: it teaches the wrong model of a channel map.
Fix: show a collision as a broken or hollow mark labelled `lost` in `--dim`, and keep hatch plus ✕ only for channels in the bad map.

C-20 [minor] owner: motion-designer — 03-motion contradicts itself in five places. (1) Phone cut 7 cross-fades three pre-rendered stills, which guardrail 4 forbids ("never a crossfade"). (2) The proof runs the 3% residual swell until P=1, so "frozen from C0" pops and "idle = 0 frames" is false inside S1. (3) A hash load straight to `/#contact` never renders the RT that S7 "re-uses". (4) `deviceMemory` exists only in Chromium browsers. (5) drei's `useFBO` returns a `WebGLRenderTarget`, which is unverified with `WebGPURenderer`.
Why it matters: the build follows this file literally.
Fix: (1) Replace cut 7 with "freeze at the AVIF focused still, no cross-fade animation". (2) Ramp the residual to 0 by P 0.9. (3) Render the RT once, deterministically (fixed t, f=1), on demand for any stop that needs it and after context loss. (4) Treat `deviceMemory` as a hint only. (5) Name `RenderTarget` from `three/webgpu`.

C-21 [minor] owner: motion-designer — performance / LCP plan: the proofs load both font families from Google Fonts, which blocks rendering. The estimated first-visit payload with three.js WebGPU, React, R3F, GSAP, Lenis, the heightfield and the fonts is about 1 MB gzipped. The full-screen poster, not the h1, is likely to become the LCP element. Building the TSL shaders is estimated to cause 300–800 ms long tasks on a phone.
Why it matters: LCP < 2.5s and INP on the pill are part of the development score.
Fix: pre-render the h1 as static HTML. Self-host and subset one Newsreader roman file (preloaded, with a size-adjusted fallback font). Keep the poster AVIF under 60 KB with `fetchpriority=high`. Request the 3D chunk after first paint, and compile with `compileAsync`, yielding between materials. Write these as numbered load-order rules in 03.

C-22 [minor] owner: ux-designer — S5-wisard: the spec has no rule for **ties**. With 3 seed classes, a 16×16 pad and a class taught from one drawing, discriminator counts will often tie or saturate at high tuple sizes. "The highest count wins" is then undefined, and the winner will look arbitrary.
Why it matters: the honesty line ("try it on your own drawings") only works if the outcome is legible.
Fix: when the top two counts are within 2, show "Too close to call: 23 vs 22", with no winner. Show the confidence gap (top minus second) as a count, which is not an accuracy claim. Announce ties in the live region.
