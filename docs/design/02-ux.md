## Research

Researched 2026-09-23. What we take and what we avoid.

| Source | What works | What fails / what we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| [NN/g: Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/) | Scroll control is OK only when it progressively discloses something valuable. It needs to sit next to normal-scroll areas, keep scroll direction the same, and carry little text. | Most participants were disoriented. Long scrolljacks read as bugs. Goal-driven users (our recruiter) "get agitated and move on". It gets worse on phones. **So we have one pin (S1, 120vh) and one scrub (S6), both short, and the text is never tied to scrub position.** |
| [Igloo Inc case study (Awwwards)](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html) | Only 3 sections. The journey was mapped before visuals. Performance was measured continuously so low-end devices could still navigate. | UI text is rendered in WebGL, and the case study describes no progress indicator or section jump. We keep the "one material world" but put all text in the DOM and add explicit orientation. |
| [Codrops: "More than a portfolio" (Apr 2026)](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/28/more-than-a-portfolio-building-a-scroll-driven-3d-world-with-something-to-say/) | Scroll reads as "one camera take". A single contact button is always visible. | It has no navbar, no stated reduced-motion path, and snap-scroll that "required extensive tuning". **We take the one-take camera and the always-visible contact. We add a map, and we avoid snapping.** |
| [Utsubo: best Three.js sites 2026](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026) | Winners pick one verb and do it cleanly. Scroll glides the camera over terrain (Primland). WebGPU with TSL fallback. | Stacking effects. We use scroll for travel and click/tap for play, and never mix the two in one place. |
| [Metabole: immersive examples 2026](https://metabole.studio/en/blog/immersive-website-examples) | Mobile-first with lighter scenes. Semantic HTML before WebGL. "Immersion serves clarity." | Every section trying to be a hero moment. We have two signature beats and the rest stays calm. |
| [Active Theory V6](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/active-theory-v6) | A minimal nav: a pill for work and contact that reacts to scroll velocity. | A velocity-reactive pill is a moving target. Our pill never moves or hides. |
| [Bruno Simon folio 2025](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025) | Play is what gets shared. | Driving to reach content fails our 30-second test. The brief rules it out. |
| [WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html) | Any drag needs a single-pointer alternative (buttons, tap-to-select, click on track). | Drag-only sims. That rules out Bubble Lock's "drag the instruction in" as the only input. |

**Takeaways that shaped this file:** (1) native scroll is the only scroll, so there's no snapping, no wheel capture and no direction change; (2) the camera moves only *between* sections and holds still while you read or poke; (3) orientation is always on screen: where you are, where else you can go, and how to get out; (4) the fast path is plain, pinned and never animated away; (5) phones get their own layout, not a squeezed desktop.

## Structure options (camera journey and navigation)

| Option | How it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Rail over the die + floorplan map** | One continuous camera spline over the atoll driven by native scroll. Each section is a normal DOM section, and the camera travels in the gap between sections and holds during each. A persistent die-map minimap gives random access, and the Surface Interrupt is the fast path. | **Chosen.** |
| B. Hub and dive | S2's die is a hub. Clicking a block zooms into a "room", and Back returns to the hub. There's no linear scroll after S2. | The rooms genre (Samsy, Cartier). Content hides behind clicks, so a passive scroller sees nothing and it fails the 30-second test. |
| C. Horizontal floorplan pan | Vertical scroll pans the camera sideways across the die. | Changes scroll direction (NN/g's worst case). The phone layout is poor, and horizontal timelines are on the overdone list. |

**Why A:** a recruiter who only scrolls still passes every block in story order, and an engineer who wants one block jumps there from the map. It's the only option that is both a one-take camera film and a scannable document. The camera path follows the floorplan **NW → centre → E → out over the SE reef → rise**, so it never backtracks.

S2 → click the CPU block on the die (or "RISC-V" in the list) → a direct camera arc to S4 → toggles forwarding and picks load-use → reads the cycle table → the section-end link "Next block: WiSARD ↓" or "Back to die map ↑" → … → S7 pads.

### 4. Phone from a LinkedIn link
Land → h1, role and the bottom bar are visible in the first viewport (at 375×667 the h1 plus role sit in the lower third, and the bar is at the bottom) → thumb on **Resume ↗**. The hero pin is 100vh, the sims are flat DOM and stacked, and the S6 scrub is 110vh. The project index is in the first viewport.

### 5. Returning, deep links, Back button
- `/#risc-v` loads with the camera already at the S4 stop and no path fly-through.
- The Surface Interrupt pushes a history entry, so Android Back or browser Back closes it and returns to the same spot.
- Section changes use `replaceState`, so Back leaves the site instead of rewinding through sections. That's the expected behaviour for a one-page site.

### 6. Jumps (map, lagoon blocks, Surface Interrupt project links)
The scroll position is set **instantly** to the target section's dwell start. The camera flies a **direct arc** from its current pose to the target stop (≤ 900ms). It doesn't replay the path, so S6's scrub and the entrance reveals along the way are not re-triggered. The DOM cross-fades, and focus moves to the target `h2` (`tabindex="-1"`). Under reduced motion or Still it's an instant cut. *(Proposed to motion-designer, see Handoffs.)*

## Scroll and camera model (the shared rules)

1. **Native scroll is the only scroll.** Lenis smoothing is fine, but there's no snapping, no wheel capture, no horizontal scroll and no velocity tricks. The document height is real, so the scrollbar is an honest progress bar.
2. **Scroll drives only the camera (travel) and S6's echo scrub.** Every sim, the map, the pads and the pill are click, tap or keyboard. Nothing inside a sim is scroll-scrubbed.
3. **Travel between sections, dwell within them.** The camera travels only in a **travel window**, while the next section's top rises from 100% to ~30% of the viewport. During that time its heading is already entering, so there's never a blank screen. Once it arrives it is in plan view on the block. In S3–S5 it then **pans 1:1 with the page** (page-locked, C-06), so the die behaves like the paper and nothing moves relative to the content. In S2 and S7 it holds.
4. **Pins:** S1 only (120vh desktop, 100vh phone). **Scrub:** S6 only (160vh desktop, 110vh phone). S3–S5 are neither pinned nor sticky (C-06). The sim is drawn on the block, and the camera pans 1:1 with the page during dwell, so every wheel tick moves the page and the die together. S6 is the only sticky split.
5. **Reversible.** Scrolling up replays everything backwards (focus melts back to shimmer), and nothing is one-way.
6. **Out of view = paused.** Sims auto-pause off-screen, in a hidden tab, and while the Surface Interrupt is open. The render loop idles when nothing changes.

Scroll budget (desktop, approximate): S1 120vh pinned → S2 ~140 → S3 ~150 → S4 ~160 → S5 ~150 → S6 160 scrubbed → S7 ~110 plus the footer. The travel windows overlap section starts, so they add no dead length.

### Shared rules for S3–S5: the sim is drawn on the block (C-06)

The old "sticky split", with the same text-left/stage-right template four times over, is **replaced**. Each sim is drawn **onto its block** in the 3D world, and each block gets **its own framing**. Only S6 keeps a side column.

**How it works (desktop and tablet, ≥768px with a fine pointer):**
- **Drawn surface:** each sim renders to its own 2D canvas. That canvas is uploaded as a `CanvasTexture` onto the block's top-metal plane, so it's lit by the scene, sits in the layer colour, and the routing at the block edge becomes its frame.
- **Input layer:** the same DOM canvas stays in the page at the exact screen rect of the drawn surface. Its bitmap is at `opacity: 0`, but it still receives pointer events and holds focus. Focus rings, hover labels (`ch14 · 2432 MHz`) and the Tide Pool's grid overlay are drawn **visibly** by the DOM layer, so keyboard users always see where they are.
- **Page-locked dwell:** so the drawn block stays under its DOM input layer, the camera does not "hold" during S3–S5. It **pans 1:1 with the page in plan view**, reading the same Lenis scroll value in the same rAF. The die moves exactly as if it were the paper the page is printed on. Relative to the content nothing moves, so the rule "no camera motion while reading or poking" still holds. Travel windows between blocks are unchanged.
- **Controls stay DOM:** buttons, sliders, radio groups, chip grids, live regions and twins are exactly as specified below, placed in a control rail on the page directly under or beside the drawn surface. The focus model is unchanged.
- **Motion's conditions (agreed):** the pan is exact for a plane (world shift = scroll px × visible world height ÷ viewport px, from the Lenis value in the same rAF, with no easing). The CanvasTexture uploads only when the sim changes (a tick, a Step, a stroke), at the DOM canvas's device-pixel size, which also keeps sim content sharp (C-07). The loop renders only while the scroll value or a sim changes. **Under reduced motion or Still, desktop uses flat-DOM too** (Lenis is off there, so native async scroll would drift).
- **Safety valve:** if measured drift between the DOM rect and the projected block exceeds 1px for 3 or more consecutive frames, that section falls back to the flat-DOM arrangement (below) until it's reloaded.

**One DOM, two skins.** Still mode is not a separate site. It's the same sections, same order, same headings and same sims, with the canvas swapped for per-section still art.

| Section | 3D mode | Still mode |
|---|---|---|
| S1-hero | live caustics, focus on scroll, 120vh pin | the **final focused frame** as an image (name lettering on the die). No pin, 100vh |
| S2-about | die plan view in canvas | **floorplan SVG** with the same clickable blocks (keyboard and screen readers still use the DOM list) |
| S3–S5 | sim drawn on the 3D block (CanvasTexture), page-locked pan | the same 2D sims **flat in the DOM** over a plan still of the block. Autoplay off, Step works |
| S6-cybot | 160vh scrub | a static echo diagram (SVG), normal height |
| S7-contact | 3D atoll, pads light on arrival | atoll SVG with all pads already lit, and the list twinning still works on hover/focus |
| Section changes | camera travel | none, or a ≤ 200ms cross-fade |
| Jumps | direct camera arc | instant scroll plus focus move |

- **Triggers:** no WebGPU and no WebGL2 → Still. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → Still visuals, watched **live** via a `change` listener. The **Still toggle** is always available both ways. saveData or deviceMemory ≤ 2 → Still with "Turn on 3D".
- With reduced motion *and* working WebGL, the 3D may render **static frames at each stop** instead of the still images, but with no travel and no caustic animation. The build decides based on cost; the layout is identical either way.
- The layout doesn't change between modes, only the backdrop. So the 30-second test result is the same in both, and the brief requires testing both.

## Usability

### Heading order and landmarks
```
<header>  wordmark (link) · "where am I" label · pill [Resume ↗][Contact]
<nav aria-label="Sections">  die-map: 7 links (Top, About, Link Layer, RISC-V, WiSARD, CyBot, Contact)
<main>
  S1 <h1>Akash Gojuru</h1>
  S2 <h2>(metaphor line)</h2>  <h3>On this die</h3>
  S3 <h2>BLE Link Layer</h2>  <h3>Try it: Hop</h3> <h3>Channel map</h3> <h3>Last 8 hops</h3>
  S4 <h2>RISC-V 5-stage…</h2> <h3>Try it: Bubble Lock</h3> <h3>Cycle table</h3>
  S5 <h2>WiSARD…</h2>        <h3>Try it: Tide Pool</h3>
  S6 <h2>CyBot…</h2>
  S7 <h2>(pads line)</h2>    <h3>Write a message</h3>
<footer>
dialog: <h2>Akash Gojuru</h2> <h3>Projects</h3>
```
The canvas is `aria-hidden="true"`, because all meaning is in the DOM. S1 carries a visually hidden one-line description of the focus moment.

### Keyboard path
Skip link → wordmark → **Resume** → **Contact** → sections nav (expands on focus) → S1 → … in visual order. Inside each sim, the order is the control rail's header buttons (Pause, Step, Reset), then scenario/toggles, then primary controls, then grids. Composite widgets (chip grid, Tide Pool grid, radio group) are **one Tab stop each**, with arrow keys inside, so a keyboard user never has to Tab through 37 chips or 256 cells. Global keys: `R` summary, `?` keys help, `Esc` closes any overlay, sheet or disclosure. Nothing traps focus except the modal dialog, and it releases on Esc.

### Focus
A double ring (agreed with art-director; the interactive colour is now **--sea #3D9DF2**, following the client's direction CLIENT-01): `outline: 3px solid var(--sea); outline-offset: 2px; box-shadow: 0 0 0 2px var(--ink #0A1016)`. Sea alone fails on pale sand (1.61:1), and the ink halo gives 10.71:1 there, so at least one edge clears 3:1 on every backdrop (sea on --floor-0 is 6.52:1). `scroll-padding-top: 72px` (desktop) and `scroll-padding-bottom: 56px + safe-area` (phone), so the pinned bars never cover focus (WCAG 2.4.11). After a jump, focus goes to the target h2. After the Surface Interrupt closes, focus returns to the trigger.

### Contrast
Body text is ≥ 4.5:1 and large text ≥ 3:1, **measured against the worst frame of the backdrop behind it**, not the average. Text over the caustic canvas always sits on a solid surface: the S1 "ink plate" (15.8:1 in every frame) and the bars on `rgba(14,19,18,.86)` (worst case over caustic white: light text 10.92:1, sand text 5.71:1). The sea CTA with ink text is 6.65:1. Cooled text tokens (art-director) on the warmest residual ground (S2, #1F292C): --light 12.69, --sand 7.06, --dim 4.70, sea 5.17. All pass. Sim states never rely on colour alone: bad channel = hatch plus ✕, bubble = the label "bubble", flushed = strike-through plus text, winning class = "→ wave" text.

### Touch
Every target is ≥ 44×44px with ≥ 8px gaps: both halves of the pill, the chips, the steps, the pad-list rows (56px), and the bottom-bar items. Nothing is hover-only; every hover twin also fires on focus and tap. The only touch-captured area on the site is the Tide Pool pad, and it has scrollable gutters. There are no custom gestures (pinch, swipe) anywhere.

### Readable without motion, readable without JS
All section text is in the HTML and visible by default. Entrance reveals are gated behind a `js` class, so if JS or WebGL fails nothing stays at opacity 0. No claim is carried only by an animation or a sim.

### No scroll trap
There's one short pin (S1) and one scrub (S6). Neither changes scroll direction or speed, and both are reversible. Wheel and trackpad scroll over every sim scrolls the page; no sim captures the wheel. The section nav and pill are always there to leave. The Surface Interrupt closes with Esc, ✕, Back or the "Back to where you were" button.

### 375px check
Every section above has a phone wireframe at 375. The only horizontal scroll is the cycle table, inside its own labelled container. The bottom bar fits: chip 144 + Resume 88 + Contact 96 + 2 gaps × 8 = 344px, inside 375 − 2 × 12px gutters = 351px. The chip label truncates with an ellipsis after 14 characters (the full name stays in its accessible name). Body text is ≥ 16px.

### Performance as UX
The first meaningful paint is HTML. The render loop pauses when idle, when the Surface Interrupt is open, and off-screen for sims. Nothing on the primary route waits for the 3D.
